WHAT YOU CAN DO

Lay the past over the present.

ImmersaMap is a free web map that overlays old maps and historical aerial photos on today's map. In Japan you can switch aerial photos by era from 1936 to today, and in the Tokyo area compare maps as old as 1896. No sign-up or app needed.

01

Overlay and compare

Old maps and aerial photos sit semi-transparently on today's map. Move the opacity slider to fade smoothly between then and now.

02

Switch eras

Switch with one tap from 1936 aerial photos to today's satellite imagery, and see how a place changed over almost a century.

03

Find your place

Jump to where you are with the location button, or search by place name or address. Topographic maps are available too, and it runs smoothly on phones.

HOW TO USE

Three simple steps.

1

Open the map

Nothing to install. Just press the button.

2

Go to a place

Use the location button, search, or drag the map.

3

Choose an era

Pick an old map or aerial photo and adjust the opacity to compare with today.

FAQ

FAQ

What is ImmersaMap?

A free web map that overlays old maps and historical aerial photos on today's map, so you can compare the same place then and now by changing the opacity.

Is it free?

Yes. No account or app is needed. It runs in your web browser for free.

Can I see what my current location looked like in the past?

Yes. On the map screen, press the location button to jump to where you are, then switch eras to see old aerial photos or maps of that place. Your browser asks before sharing your location.

Which years of aerial photos are available?

For Japan, from the 1930s (1936–1942) to today (2004 onward), switchable by era. Older eras cover limited areas such as Tokyo and Osaka; from the 1960s most of the country is covered. Satellite imagery is also available for the latest era. Aerial photos of some European places are in the prototype.

Which old maps are available?

For the Tokyo area: topographic maps from the late Meiji era (1896–1909), the Taisho era (1917–1924), the early Showa era (1927–1939) and the post-war years (1944–1954).

Does it work on smartphones?

Yes. It works in the browser on smartphones, tablets and computers.

Where does the data come from?

Base map: OpenStreetMap. Aerial photos and topographic maps: GSI Japan tiles. Latest satellite imagery: Esri World Imagery. Old maps from the Meiji era to the post-war years: "Konjaku Map on the Web" by Kenji Tani, Saitama University.

ABOUT

About ImmersaMap

ImmersaMap is an independent web map project. It began with a simple wish: anyone should be able to check right away what a place looked like in the past. It brings materials such as government aerial photos and Meiji-era topographic maps into a form you can use without expert knowledge, so the history of familiar streets feels close at hand.
